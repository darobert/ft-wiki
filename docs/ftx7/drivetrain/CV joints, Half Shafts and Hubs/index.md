# Rear Axle Assembly

The rear axle assembly consists of...
- Inner CV joint
- Half shaft
- Outer CV joint
- Wheel hub

For FTX-7, similarly to FTX-6, the rear axle assembly was taken from a Mazda MX-5.

More specifically, they were taken from a 1989-93 MX-5 1.6L NA. This is because this MX-5 model has bolt on inner CV joints, rather than ones that spline directly into a differential.

This was opted for because since a spool was being used, bolting directly to a spool flange would be much easier and cost effective than machining splines into the spool.

Should a fresh set of axles need to be bought, they were purchased off DoneDeal from Robert Maguire who had a lot of scrap MX5s. His profile is linked below.

[DoneDeal – MX-5 parts seller](https://www.donedeal.ie/all?userId=5174965)

## Design considerations

The MX5s that the half shafts were sourced from subject the rear axle to almost exactly double the torque that the FTX-7 CBR600RR after gear reductions, so the strength of the half shafts is not a concern.

Keep in mind since FTX-7 used a jacking setup, *theoretically* each half shaft can be subjected to 100% of the engine torque (one wheel lifts fully off the ground), rather than 50% in a usual case. In practical applications this would not happen, but any calculations done regarding half shaft strength should assume it does as a conservative case.

As with any bolted component on the car, bolt shear, tear-out and bearing must be considered, again using worst case loading of pure jacking.

## What could be improved

MX-5 rear axle assembly is quite heavy, a lighter option could be considered.

When cutting half shafts to length, they were cut in half, turned to 45 degree ends at the end of each half on a lathe, and a small circular key and slot were created on each side of the shaft. Feedback from Head of Manufacturing recommened that in future the ends be cut to a 30 degree angle with a sharp point rather than a slot and key in order to improve weld penetration.
