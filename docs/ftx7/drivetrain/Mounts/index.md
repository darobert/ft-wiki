# Drivetrain Mounts

## FTX-7 Mount Design Choices

- The drivetrain mounts were CNC cut from 3.0 mm mild steel plate.

- For the right-hand drivetrain mount, the critical additional design requirement was the brake caliper mounting location. The maximum braking force was determined using a deliberately conservative rear brake bias. In reality, the majority of the braking force is reacted through the front axle due to longitudinal load transfer under braking.

- The tangential braking force acting at the brake disc was determined from the maximum braking torque:

$$
F_{\text{brake}} = \frac{T_{\text{brake}}}{r_{\text{disc}}}
$$

where:

- $F_{\text{brake}}$ = tangential force at the brake disc
- $T_{\text{brake}}$ = braking torque
- $r_{\text{disc}}$ = effective brake disc radius

- This force was then used to verify the principal failure modes associated with the caliper mounting bolts and surrounding mount material:
  - Bolt shear
  - Bearing failure at the bolt holes
  - Edge tear-out / shear-out
  - Adequate material ligament around the mounting holes

### Chain Load and Bearing Reactions

- For the mount without the brake caliper, the critical loading condition was associated with the spool bearing mounting points. Chain tension acting on the driven sprocket produces a radial load on the spool, which must ultimately be reacted through the two spool bearings and their respective drivetrain mounts.

- The chain force was determined from the maximum torque transmitted through the driven sprocket:

$$
F_{\text{chain}} = \frac{T_{\text{sprocket}}}{r_{\text{sprocket}}}
$$

where:

- $F_{\text{chain}}$ = effective chain force
- $T_{\text{sprocket}}$ = torque at the driven sprocket
- $r_{\text{sprocket}}$ = sprocket pitch radius

The torque transmitted by a chain drive is generated by the difference between the tight-side and slack-side chain tensions:

$$
T_{\text{sprocket}} =
(F_{\text{tight}} - F_{\text{slack}})
r_{\text{sprocket}}
$$


### Bearing Reactions

- The spool can be treated as a rigid beam supported by its two bearings. Using static equilibrium, the reaction force at each bearing can be calculated from the sprocket load and its position relative to the bearing centres.

For a sprocket positioned between two bearings:

$$
R_L + R_R = F_{\text{chain}}
$$

Taking moments about the left bearing:

$$
R_R L = F_{\text{chain}} a
$$


$$
R_R = F_{\text{chain}}\frac{a}{L}
$$


$$
R_L = F_{\text{chain}}\frac{L-a}{L}
$$

where:

- $R_L$ = reaction force at the left bearing
- $R_R$ = reaction force at the right bearing
- $L$ = distance between bearing centres
- $a$ = distance from the left bearing to the sprocket load

- A shear force diagram (SFD) provides a useful method of visualising and verifying these bearing reactions. The calculated reactions can then be applied to the drivetrain mounts for analytical checks and FEA.

### Spool Bending

- A bending moment diagram (BMD) should also be produced for the spool. This allows the maximum bending moment and its location to be identified.

- The resulting bending stress in the spool can be determined using:

$$
\sigma_b = \frac{My}{I}
$$

where:

- $\sigma_b$ = bending stress
- $M$ = bending moment
- $y$ = distance from the neutral axis to the outer surface
- $I$ = second moment of area

- Increasing the unsupported distance between the sprocket and its nearest bearing increases the bending moment induced in the spool. Minimising this distance reduces spool bending and helps maintain drivetrain alignment.

## What Could Be Improved

- Outside the design requirements described above, much of the FTX-7 mount geometry was dictated by the existing engine and chassis packaging rather than by an intentionally optimised load path.

- In particular, supporting the drivetrain from the engine resulted in indirect load paths between the spool bearings and the chassis. This required additional material to achieve sufficient stiffness and strength, contributing to unnecessarily heavy drivetrain mounts.

- FEA was used during the FTX-7 design process to identify low-stress regions and remove unnecessary material. However, further mass reduction would likely have been possible.

- A future design should first improve the fundamental load paths before using FEA for geometry optimisation. 

- Future drivetrain designs should aim to transfer bearing reactions into the chassis through the shortest and stiffest practical load paths. This should allow substantially lighter mounts while maintaining adequate stiffness, strength and drivetrain alignment.

- Could be possible to make mounts from a more thin steel sheet? Mount stiffness should be considered alongside strength. Excessive deflection at the spool bearings can cause sprocket and chain misalignment even when stresses remain below yield.

- FEA should therefore be used to verify both maximum stress and displacement at the bearing locations under the critical drivetrain load cases.

### Engine-Mounted Drivetrain

Mounting the drivetrain assembly directly from the engine should be avoided in future designs. This arrangement created several practical and structural disadvantages:

- **Poor serviceability:** Removing the engine also required removal of the drivetrain assembly, significantly increasing the work required for maintenance.

- **Poor repeatability:** The engine did not return to exactly the same position and orientation following removal and reinstallation.

- **Mounting-hole misalignment:** Small changes in engine position consequently changed the relative position of the drivetrain mounts, meaning mounting holes frequently failed to align correctly during reassembly.

- **Drivetrain alignment:** Variability in engine and drivetrain position made consistent sprocket and chain alignment more difficult to maintain.

- **Inefficient load paths:** Bearing and chain loads had to pass through the drivetrain mounts and engine mounting structure before reaching the chassis, rather than being reacted directly into the chassis.

For future cars, the drivetrain should therefore be located and supported directly from well-defined chassis hard points wherever practical. The engine and drivetrain should be independently removable, with repeatable locating features used to maintain spool, sprocket and chain alignment between assemblies.
  
- Drivetrain should be mounted off top and bottom bars of the rear bulkhead.
